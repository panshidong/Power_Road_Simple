4.2 Task A – Resilient Strategy Comparison: Criticality-Based Analysis
Building on the interdependent recovery simulator in Section 3.1 and the study inputs defined in Section 4.1, Task A provides a controlled comparison of restoration strategies in an interdependent power–road setting. The main outcome is the system-level resilience, quantified by the resilience-triangle area computed from the simulated restoration trajectory. 
In practice, agencies often rely on priority rules and pre-established rankings that can be applied rapidly either for pre-event effort or post-event recovery efforts. Accordingly, Task A focuses on criticality-based repair sequences rather than solving a fully joint, event-specific optimization problem for every scenario; a complete joint optimization would make a “repair everything jointly” strategy superior by construction but would provide limited insight into the value of alternative prioritization rules which are typical practices by agencies. 
With this scope, Task A evaluates three strategy classes that differ in how priorities are applied across networks: (S0) repair power first, (S1) repair roads first, and (S2) repair power and roads together using a single integrated ranking. Within this structure, two sequence-construction pathways will be examined: (1) compute criticality scores separately on the power and road networks and then stack the resulting ranked lists to represent S0 and S1; and (2) compute combined-network criticality scores on an integrated interdependence representation to define S2. 
To generate the criticality scores used in these sequences, Task A will consider multiple metric families commonly used in infrastructure analysis, including standard centrality measures (e.g., betweenness-type indices) and Shapley-value-inspired indices (Equation 1) that allocate service-based performance gains to individual components. As we are using heterogeneous networks like roadway (link being component) and power (node being component), a general metric may not be applicable for be joint network topology, therefore we will only use them where applicable. In the Shapley-based formulation, the characteristic function is defined using service outcomes consistent with the simulator outputs, so that component importance reflects interdependency-relevant contributions rather than topology alone.

Since exact Shapley computation becomes expensive for large component sets, Shapley-based scores will be estimated using sampling over repair orderings. The sampling configuration will be reported, and the analysis will check that the resulting rankings are reasonably stable under changes in sampling effort. 
Because recovery outcomes can be case-sensitive and depend on damage pattern, Task A will evaluate strategies across a broad set of disruption scenarios defined under a random scenario-generation procedure. This design is intended to distinguish systematic strategy differences from scenario-specific ones. 
Task A will report resilience-triangle outcomes to evaluate how (i) isolated versus integrated sequencing and (ii) the choice of criticality metric affect recovery efficiency. These results address RQ1 by assessing whether more interdependence-aware criticality definitions yield meaningfully different recovery trajectories than simpler indices under the same sequencing structure; in addition, the heuristic-based optimal sequence from Section 3.1 will be reported as a reference for context rather than as a guaranteed optimum.
